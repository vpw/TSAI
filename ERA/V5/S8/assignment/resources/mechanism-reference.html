The frame

Nearly every mechanism here is paying down one of two bills

Standard attention works. It is also the most expensive thing in the model, in two independent ways — and almost every entry below is someone deciding which of the two to attack, and what accuracy they are willing to trade for it.

Bill 1 — quadratic compute

~T² score comparisons

Every token scores against every other. Double the context, quadruple the work. Paid at training and at prefill, and it is compute, so it can be bought with more hardware.

Bill 2 — linear KV-cache memory

2 × layers × kv_heads × head_dim × T × batch × bytes

At decode time every past key and value must stay resident. Only linear in T — but it is per user and unshared, so it multiplies by your concurrency. At the lesson's yardstick (48 layers, 8 KV heads, head_dim 128, bf16, T=32,768) that is 6.44 GB for one user, 51.54 GB for eight.

A third bill nobody named in class: memory bandwidth — how many times the score matrix crosses between slow HBM and fast on-chip SRAM. FlashAttention (#11) attacks only that one, which is why it is the single entry here that costs no accuracy at all. It is the bonus mechanism for exactly that reason.

Patterns worth noticing

Four threads that only show up in date order

The memory question was asked twice, four years apart

MQA lands in 2019 and then essentially nothing happens on the KV cache until GQA in 2023. The answer was available the whole time; the field only cared once serving cost, not training cost, became the binding constraint. MLA (2024) and attention sinks (2023) cluster in that same late wave.

The delta rule sat unused for three years

Schlag et al. publish it in Feb 2021 — before RoPE. It goes quiet until Yang et al. make it trainable in parallel in 2024, and only then does it become an architecture. The gap is not about the idea; it is about the training algorithm. Good ideas wait for their hardware-shaped implementation.

Position keeps getting re-solved, never solved

Learned table (2016) → sinusoidal (2017) → RoPE (2021) → ALiBi (2021) → NTK-Aware and NTK-By-Parts (2023) → YaRN (2023) → DroPE (2025). Eight attempts across nine years, and the most recent answer is to remove positional embeddings entirely. That trajectory — add, refine, refine, delete — is the single most legible arc on the timeline.

The quiet stretch was not quiet

Between ALiBi (Aug 2021) and GQA (May 2023) the minimum-coverage list shows almost nothing. FlashAttention (May 2022) sits right in that gap. The field was working on the systems half — how fast can you compute the thing you already have — while the architectural half paused. Exactness stayed affordable longer than it otherwise would have.
