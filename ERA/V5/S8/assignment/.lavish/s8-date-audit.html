Where I would push back if I were reviewing this

The five attribution calls, plus one open build detail

On each of these the date is not in question — the paper we point at really was published on the day we say. What is in question is whether that paper is the right one to point at. I have taken a position on each; flag the row if you disagree and I will go verify the challenger properly before anything is written into the data file.

1 · Learned absolute positions → ConvS2S

The call: credit the paper Attention Is All You Need itself credits. Its text says "We also experimented with using learned positional embeddings [9]", and [9] is ConvS2S.

The challenge: the same group used position embeddings in an earlier convolutional NMT paper (2016). Flag this row and I will check whether that earlier paper introduces them or merely uses them — I have not verified it, and I would rather say so than let a guess into the table.

4 · Sparse / top-k → Sparse Transformers

The call: 2019-04-23 is the earliest widely-credited source for factorized sparse attention patterns cutting full O(T²).

The challenge: restricted/local attention patterns appear in 2018 image-transformer work. Whether that counts as "sparse attention" or as a precursor is a definitional call, not a factual one — worth a sentence in the dossier either way.

6 · Sliding window → Longformer

The call: Longformer, 2020-04-10.

Two challenges, opposite directions: Sparse Transformers (2019) already had local patterns, so Longformer may be late; and Mistral 7B (2023) is what most people associate the mechanism with, so Longformer may look early. The app should say explicitly that Mistral is the famous deployment, not the origin — that misattribution is common enough to be worth correcting on the page.

7 · Linear attention → Transformers are RNNs

The call: 2020-06-29, the standard credited source for the kernel-feature-map reformulation that turns attention into a fixed-size recurrent state.

The challenge: earlier softmax-free linear-complexity attention work exists (2018–2019). This row's framing matters because the lesson builds its whole no-softmax argument on it — flag it and I will check the earlier candidates rather than leaning on "standard credited source".

8 vs 17 · Delta rule priority already flagged

The call: two separate rows, three years apart. Schlag et al. 2021-02-22 originate the delta-rule correction on a linear-attention fast-weight state; Yang et al. 2024-06-10 contribute the parallel-scan training algorithm, and are what everyone means by "the DeltaNet paper".

This is the table's best find and I want to keep it visible rather than resolve it: the 2021 origin even predates RoPE. The timeline draws a priority arc between the two, which is the whole point.

13 · NTK-Aware vs NTK-By-Parts open build detail

The call, provisionally: one marker, dated 2023-06-29, with the distinction written into the dossier.

bloc97 published two methods, and YaRN builds on NTK-By-Parts, not on plain NTK-Aware. The brief's minimum list names only "NTK-aware scaling", so one marker satisfies it — but two markers would make the YaRN lineage arrow point at the right thing. This is the one place I would take a different answer. Flag the row if you want two.
