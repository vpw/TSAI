What your flags turned up

Each of these was a case where the date was solid but "who was first" was my judgment. I went to the primary sources. One row moved, one row split into two, and three stand — but they now stand on the papers' own words rather than on my summary of them.

#1 · Learned positions date corrected

2017-05-08 → 2016-11-07. You were right to flag it. I had credited the paper Attention Is All You Need cites — but AIAYN cites the later of two papers by the same group.

The earlier one settles it. 1611.02344 says outright: "we add position embeddings to encode the absolute position of each source word within a sentence", with the form e_j = w_j + l_j. That is the mechanism, six months before ConvS2S.

And it points further back. The same passage credits Sukhbaatar et al. 2015 (memory networks). Checking 1503.08895: its "position encoding" is fixed, but its separate "Temporal Encoding" matrix is explicitly "learned during training" — a learned position embedding, except it indexes memory slots, not token positions. Different granularity, so the marker stays at 1611.02344 and the dossier states the antecedent.

#13 → #13 + #13b · NTK split, as you asked

NTK-By-Parts is now its own marker, dated 2023-07-07. It turned out not to be a Reddit post at all — YaRN's bibliography points at a GitHub pull request, jquesnelle/scaled-rope#1.

Which makes it the best-evidenced non-paper row in the table. GitHub's own API gives two independent timestamps: the PR opened at 2023-07-07T20:40:33Z by bloc97, and its first commit authored 2023-07-07T20:24:12Z. Platform record, not archival inference.

The third method — decided: dossier text only. YaRN cites a "Dynamic NTK" method by emozilla (a different author) as the other improvement on NTK-Aware. Your ruling is that it stays in the NTK dossier prose and gets no timeline marker — which is also the defensible call on the evidence: it is not in the brief's minimum-coverage list, and I could not date it to the standard every other row here meets. Plotting a marker on a date I could not verify would breach the one rule this audit exists to enforce.

#7 · Linear attention stands — challenger verified

This was your strongest flag, and the challenger is real. Shen et al., "Efficient Attention: Attention with Linear Complexities", arXiv 1812.01243, v1 2018-12-04 — linear-complexity attention 19 months earlier. Katharopoulos et al. cite it and call it "concurrent", which is fair for publication venues and not for arXiv v1 dates.

What keeps the row where it is: the lesson's argument is about attention becoming an RNN with a fixed-size recurrent state — and that needs the causal formulation. Efficient Attention is a vision paper. A full-text scan finds exactly one occurrence of "causal" or "autoregressive" in the entire PDF, and it is inside a bibliography entry. It never builds the recurrence. So: marker stays at 2020-06-29, dossier names Shen et al. as the earlier linear-complexity attention.

#4 · Sparse attention stands — related work summarised

You asked for a synopsis of the paper's Related Work. It is short and it does the opposite of claiming primacy — it names what came before: "(Parmar et al., 2018) uses blocks of local attention to apply Transformers to images" (Image Transformer, v1 2018-02-15, ~14 months earlier), Transformer-XL for state reuse, plus Reed, Menick & Kalchbrenner and Huang for parallel, multi-scale and relative-attention work.

Its own positioning is breadth and simplicity: "Our work is simpler than many of the techniques above and can be applied equally across images, text, and audio. Many of the above techniques are orthogonal to ours." So the app credits it for factorized sparse attention at scale, and names Parmar et al. for local-block attention.

#6 · Sliding window stands — with Longformer's own caveat

Longformer does not claim the pattern either. Its own words: "The model with the most similar attention pattern to ours is Sparse Transformer (Child et al., 2019), which uses a form of dilated sliding window of blocks of size 8x8".

Its stated contributions are a more flexible CUDA kernel and "additional task motivated global attention patterns… essential for good performance in the transfer learning setting". So the honest credit is sliding window + global tokens as a named NLP mechanism — not the window itself, which is already in row #4's paper.

#8 vs #17 · Delta rule priority unchanged

Left exactly as it was, because it is already the table's best find and it is already visible: Schlag et al. 2021-02-22 originate the delta-rule correction (before RoPE); Yang et al. 2024-06-10 contribute the parallel-scan training algorithm and are what everyone means by "the DeltaNet paper". Two rows, three years apart, with a priority arc drawn between them on the timeline.

The general lesson, worth carrying into the README. "The paper the famous paper cites" is a good heuristic and not a proof — it tells you what those authors reached for, not what came first. Row #1 was wrong for exactly that reason, and rows #4, #6 and #7 all turned out to have precursors that the credited papers themselves name in their related-work sections. Reading those sections is the cheapest possible check, and it is the one I should have run before calling any of these settled.
