The timeline is the argument, not the decoration

Chronology research is done and verified — 20 rows, primary sources, arXiv v1 dates. This is the plan for what gets built on top of it: one page, five acts, five widgets, one data file. Review and annotate anything; four open decisions are at the bottom with controls to send answers back.

Here is the part I actually care about, and it is not the animations… When you lay them out on a timeline you can watch the field change its mind. — S8 assignment brief

The one idea

Every mechanism is scored against the same two bills, on a real time axis

The failure mode for this assignment is 20 pretty cards in date order. That's still a list — it just happens to be sorted. The fix is two structural commitments:

Commitment 1

Proportional time, not evenly-spaced cards

Markers sit at their true position on a continuous axis. Clusters look like clusters and gaps look like gaps — including the empty stretch through all of 2018, and the 21-month quiet between ALiBi and GQA. The gaps carry as much of the answer to Q2 as the entries do, and even spacing destroys them.

Commitment 2

Four lanes = which bill it pays

Every marker sits in the lane for the problem it attacks. The field "changing its mind" then becomes something you literally see — activity jumping lane to lane over the years — rather than something the copy has to assert.

Act 3 · the repeated unit

The dossier template, mocked in the real design system

Filled with real content for one entry so the shape can be judged rather than imagined.

The honesty layer is a feature, not an appendix. The brief's one explicit warning is about invented dates, and it says the sources are "the part that is easiest to check." Putting how each date was verified on the face of every dossier — including the two entries where the honest answer is uncomfortable — is the cheapest way to be visibly right. The two uncomfortable ones:

  • NTK-aware scaling has no paper — but as of your annotation it now has an exact date. It is a Reddit post by u/bloc97. Following the permalink you supplied: reddit.com itself is bot-walled (both curl and a real browser get "Prove your humanity"), so the date came from the Wayback Machine two ways — a snapshot of the post carries Reddit's own created-timestamp="2023-06-29T08:21:29Z" with author="bloc97", and Wayback's earliest capture of that URL is 08:21:50 UTC the same day, 21 seconds later, which independently bounds it because an archive cannot predate what it archived. 2023-06-29, no longer approximate — the marker above is now solid. Still worth flagging in the app that the primary source is a forum post, not a paper.
  • There are two bloc97 methods, not one. Found while chasing that date: arXiv 2401.07004's reference list carries both "NTK-Aware Scaled RoPE" and "Add NTK-Aware interpolation by parts correction" (NTK-By-Parts) as separate entries — and YaRN builds on NTK-By-Parts, not on plain NTK-Aware. Open question for the build: one marker or two? I lean one marker with the distinction stated in the dossier, since the brief's minimum list names only "NTK-aware scaling."
  • The delta rule's priority is disputed. Schlag et al. 2021 (2102.11174) predates the paper usually cited as "the DeltaNet paper" (Yang et al. 2024) by over three years; the 2024 contribution is the parallel-scan training algorithm, not the rule. Both get markers, joined by an arc.

Act 4 · draft answer to the bonus question

Seven things visible on the axis that a list cannot show

These are the claims the app will make and let you verify by pointing at the timeline. Annotate any you disagree with — this is the 1000-point answer, so it is worth arguing about now.

2017–2019

The field used attention for two years before it started paying for it

All of 2018 is empty. The first mechanism aimed at attention's cost rather than its capability is Sparse Transformers in April 2019 — nearly two years after the Transformer. 2018 was spent scaling the vanilla thing, not fixing it. A list has no way to show a year in which nothing happened.

position lane

Position was solved twice, and the second time was retroactive

The position lane is bimodal: 2017 and 2021 solve it for models you are about to train; 2023 and 2025 (NTK-aware, YaRN, DroPE) solve it for models already trained. An entire sub-field exists only because RoPE made position a function instead of a table — you can't retrofit a lookup table, but you can rescale a rotation. The second cluster is a consequence of the first, four years later.

2019 → 2023

Invention date and adoption date are different dates

MQA lands in November 2019 and then nothing in the memory lane for three and a half years. It wasn't a bad idea in 2019 — it was an idea whose bill nobody was paying yet. GQA in 2023 is essentially MQA with the quality loss softened, arriving exactly when serving cost started to dominate. The gap is the finding.

2021 → 2024

Ideas can arrive before the hardware that makes them trainable

The delta rule sat in the literature from February 2021 and became practical in June 2024, when someone worked out how to parallelize it across sequence length. Three years of dormancy, then two papers in seven months. A chronological view makes the lag legible; a taxonomy would file both under "linear attention" and lose it entirely.

2022

The quiet stretch is only quiet if you ignore the kernels

Between ALiBi (Aug 2021) and GQA (May 2023) the minimum-coverage list is empty for 21 months. FlashAttention (May 2022) fills it — and it is the one mechanism here that changes nothing about what attention computes, only how many times the matrix crosses the memory bus. The field alternates between "what should we compute" and "how fast can we compute what we have," and buying headroom the second way is part of why exact attention survived as long as it did.

whole axis

The oscillation the brief predicted is real, and it is accelerating

Exactness (2017) → decode memory (2019) → length (2020–21) → systems (2022) → memory again (2023) → length again (2023) → memory and compute jointly (2024–25). The early swings take years; by 2023 four mechanisms land in four months. The later entries also stop picking one bill: MLA and NSA attack cache and compute at once, which is what "guess what comes next" should be read off — the next mechanisms are unlikely to be single-bill.

2017-05 vs 2017-06

The Transformer adopted position, it didn't invent it

Learned absolute positional embeddings are five weeks older than "Attention Is All You Need," which cites them. The very first row of the timeline is already a correction to the way the story is usually told — and it is exactly the kind of thing you only notice when you are forced to put a date on everything.

Interactivity budget

Five widgets, each shared across several mechanisms

"A clear static page beats a broken clever one." So the budget is capped at five, chosen for coverage per widget rather than one toy per mechanism — and each one is governed by a hard rule below.

The caption rule (non-negotiable): every widget's conclusion is written into its static caption. If JavaScript fails, the reader still learns the point and the page still reads as a finished explainer. This is the insurance policy against "broken clever."

Implementation

One data file, three consumers, no build step

Vanilla HTML/CSS/JS. No framework, no bundler, no dependency that can rot between now and the deadline. Design tokens inherited from S7's site/index.html, so the two submissions read as one body of work.

The single-source-of-truth rule matters more than the stack. Each date appears in three places — timeline marker, dossier header, sources table — plus a fourth in the README. All four read from data/mechanisms.js. Nothing is retyped, so nothing can drift.

  • index.html — structure and all static prose
  • app.css — tokens, light/dark, layout
  • app.js — timeline renderer + the five widgets
  • data/mechanisms.js — the 20 rows, verbatim from the research doc
  • tools/gen-readme.mjs — regenerates the README source table

Deploy: host is deliberately left open — static folder, no env vars, no server, so Netlify, Vercel and ht-ml.app are all one command apart and the incognito check passes by construction on any of them. Repo goes out by subtree split to github.com/vpw/era-v5-s8, per the standing convention.

Tooling · asked about explicitly

Where /lavish and the Chrome skill fit

One correction first. /lavish is a real slash command — that's what produced this page. /chrome is not. The installed skill is chrome-devtools-axi and it is marked user-invocable: false, so it has no slash command; it activates automatically whenever a task needs a real browser. (There is also a separate claude-in-chrome skill with MCP tools, but chrome-devtools-axi's own guidance says to prefer it, and that's what I'd use.) Nothing to change on your side — just don't expect /chrome to autocomplete.

Answering your question — what "assert the bill meter at the yardstick" means. "The yardstick" is the lesson's own fixed reference configuration from §10: 48 layers, 8 KV heads, head_dim 128, bf16, T = 32,768, one user. Put those into the cache formula and the lesson states the answer: 6.44 GB for one user, 51.54 GB for eight.

So the check is: drive the widget's slider to T = 32,768, read the number it displays via eval, and confirm it says 6.44 GB. If it says anything else, the widget's arithmetic is wrong — and since every mechanism in the app is scored on that same meter, a quiet off-by-one there would corrupt every comparison the app makes. It's a regression test against a number the lesson already published, which is what makes it worth automating rather than eyeballing.

Confirming the arithmetic: 2 × 48 × 8 × 128 × 32,768 × 2 bytes = 6,442,450,944 bytes = 6.44 GB.

Hosting — updated per your note. Agreed, and I've dropped the "Netlify is the submission link" claim: the brief says "Netlify or Vercel or wherever you like," so the real requirements are just shareable, works in incognito, and renders correctly. ht-ml.app via lavish-axi share qualifies — shares are public by default, which is exactly what the incognito check wants.

Since the app is a static folder with no build step, all three hosts are one command apart and nothing in the plan depends on the choice. Suggestion: build first, then publish to whichever is least friction on the day — with share as a zero-setup fallback if a Netlify/Vercel login gets in the way near the deadline. Worth noting ht-ml.app is a third-party host and the plan's multi-file layout would need inlining, which share does automatically for local assets.

Risks

What could go wrong, and what absorbs it

Date drift across copies

The exact failure the brief warns about, arriving by accident rather than by hallucination. Absorbed by: one data file feeding all four surfaces, plus the phase-1 audit artifact.

Widget scope creep eats the deadline

Twenty mechanisms is a lot of surface to animate. Absorbed by: the five-widget cap and the caption rule — prose first, widget as enhancement, so the page is submittable at any point after Act 3 lands.

Proportional axis crushes 2017–2019

True spacing means early labels collide and late ones pile up. Absorbed by: lanes separate vertically, labels alternate above/below, and a zoom/scrub control if collisions survive that. Mobile flips to vertical with proportional gaps kept.

The approximate NTK date gets laundered closed

Was the one date I couldn't make exact. Resolved by your annotation: pinned to 2023-06-29 via the post's own embedded timestamp in a Wayback snapshot, corroborated by the earliest capture 21 seconds later. All 20 rows now carry exact, sourced dates and the risk is retired.
