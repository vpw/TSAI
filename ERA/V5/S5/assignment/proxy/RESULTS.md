## Decision rules, as declared

**1. Mixture vs the web-heavy default — REFUTED on the general-web clause**

- verified Indic: A is 0.3293 bpb better than B, σ=0.2588 → passes (only 1.27× the noise band — thin)
- agentic: A is 0.1816 bpb better than B, σ=0.0236 → passes (7.7× the noise band)
- general web cost: +7.28% relative against a 2% budget → **FAILS**

  The declared rule makes this fatal, so the arm is reported as refuted. Worth
  saying plainly what happened: arm B trains on 72% web against arm A's 32%, so B
  modelling web better was never in doubt — the 2% budget was set before the
  effect size was known and was simply too tight. That is a badly chosen
  threshold, not a surprise about the mixture, and the honest move is to report
  the rule as failed and quote the real price: **7.3% general-web
  bpb is what the capability lanes cost.** The threshold is not rewritten after
  the fact.

**2. Does the protected floor earn its cost — YES**

- removing it costs **0.8587 bpb on verified Indic** (σ=0.2588, 3.3× the noise band)
- removing it costs **0.1584 bpb on agentic** (σ=0.0236, 6.7× the noise band)
- and 1.5071 bpb on `indic_B_unverified` (178× σ)
- and 0.6676 bpb on `indic_C_translated` (70× σ)
- and 1.3996 bpb on `indic_D_synthetic` (177× σ)

  What the floor costs, on the lanes it takes batch share away from:

  | Lane | A (floor on) | C (floor off) | price of the floor | vs σ |
  |---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
  | `general_web` | 1.5074 | 1.4750 | 0.0324 | 2.3× |
  | `code` | 1.0240 | 0.9902 | 0.0338 | 2.1× |
  | `stem` | 1.6012 | 1.5673 | 0.0339 | 1.6× |
  | `reasoning` | 1.0990 | 1.0722 | 0.0269 | 1.8× |
  | `long_context` | 1.4865 | 1.4516 | 0.0349 | 2.5× |

  The floor is not free — every unprotected lane is measurably worse with it on — but
  the price is ~0.03 bpb each while the Indic lanes move by 0.9 to 1.5. That ratio,
  not the sign, is the argument for the floor.

**3. Four-tier Indic split vs verified-only — NO SIGNAL AT THIS SCALE**

- on held-out verified Indic, A is 0.0387 bpb worse than D, against σ=0.2588 on that lane
- **0.0387 is 0.15× the seed-noise band, so this
  comparison decides nothing.** The verified-Indic lane is the noisiest in the
  experiment (σ 18× the median), because its validation set is the smallest and
  its content the most heterogeneous. Neither the four-tier split nor
  verified-only is supported over the other here; the rule is untestable at this
  scale rather than failed. A single-seed run would have 'shown' whichever
  direction its seed happened to land on.

  Supplementary — the tiers arm D never sees (not part of the declared rule):

  | Indic lane | A | D | A − D |
  |---|---:|---:|---:|
  | `indic_A_verified` | 1.4427 | 1.4040 | 0.0387 |
  | `indic_B_unverified` | 0.6939 | 0.6984 | -0.0045 |
  | `indic_C_translated` | 1.1016 | 1.1732 | -0.0716 |
  | `indic_D_synthetic` | 0.6821 | 0.8014 | -0.1193 |

  A negative last column means arm A is better on that tier. Arm D trains on none of
  the unverified, translated or synthetic tiers, so any gap there is the coverage the
  four-tier split buys — which held-out *verified* perplexity alone cannot see.

**4. Transition stability — NOT REPRODUCED AT THIS SCALE**

The reported ratio is peak grad norm in the 300 steps after the switch, over the
median of the 200 before. Reading that alone would say the seam is unstable. It is
not enough on its own, so the last column applies the *same* statistic to settled
training far away from the seam — steps 100→switch−200 and switch+300→end, with the
initialisation transient excluded:

| Probe | embeddings | warmup | peak ratio at the seam | same statistic, no seam nearby |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `E1_hard_frozen` | **frozen** | 0 steps | 3.63× | 3.24× |
| `E2_hard_trainable` | trainable | 0 steps | 2.89× | 2.58× |
| `E3_warm_trainable` | trainable | 114 steps | 3.50× | 3.47× |

Settled training with no mixture change anywhere near it already produces 2.6-3.5×
excursions. The seam is the single largest gradient of the run in all three probes,
but only by 1-15%, and two of the three *background* figures are themselves over the
3× threshold. **At 40M parameters the peak-ratio-against-3× test does not
discriminate a mixture transition from ordinary gradient noise.**

- **Frozen vs trainable embeddings**, identical shift: 3.63× vs 2.89×. The direction matches the widget's claim, but the effect is not
  specific to the seam: the frozen run also runs hotter *away* from the seam, so
  what is measured is a whole-run property of freezing embeddings, not a
  transition spike it causes. The widget's 151× vs 8.0× (a 19× gap) does not
  appear here; this is a 1.26× gap.
- **Warmup band**: 3.50× with a 10% band against 2.89× for the
  same configuration with a hard step. The band did not lower the peak — it was
  slightly higher. **Refuted at this scale.**

None of this refutes the practice at 120B, where embeddings carry far more of the
representation and mixture shifts are much larger in absolute tokens. It does say the
proxy cannot be used as evidence for it, and the plan's warmup-band commitment rests
on the session's measurement and V4's production experience, not on this run.
